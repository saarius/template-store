# Native product retirement and return

## Verified scope · November 14, 2026
A disposable copy of Backroad Bum's actual EmDash1.4.0 / signed Registry Commerce0.8.0 install exercised Archive, Restore and page republishing through native controls. The live catalog was never archived or republished; the theme, cart and checkout contracts are unchanged. Evidence: `evidence/2026-11-14-product-lifecycle/`; [Commerce276](https://github.com/dinkuskit/commerce/issues/276) owns the missing post-Restore publishing guidance, not an accepted repair. This is cached installed-plugin proof, not a fresh signature/download or provider qualification.

## State is split deliberately
- **Archive product → Archive confirmation** sets Commerce's archived state. It leaves the public catalog, Shop and product lookup; the merchant product route returns 404. It does not unpublish or delete CMS content, prices, pictures or paid Orders.
- **Restore** returns the Commerce product as a **Draft**. Do not interpret the CMS's existing **Live version** summary as proof that Commerce is selling it. In this installed stack the page has no new content draft, native **Saved** is disabled and there is no Publish button; the shopper route stays 404. This is consistent with the accepted Restore→Draft contract, but the instruction to publish is not actionable from that state alone.
- **Observed native workaround:** press **Unpublish Product** once, then **Publish now**, then its native confirmation. The unpublish press acts immediately in EmDash1.4.0—do not wait for or assume an extra confirmation. The page's actual publication check, not a catalog patch, puts the product back on the shop. Native reload then shows Commerce **On sale**, and the same public product returns 200.

Use that publication transition only for an intentionally approved product return. Snapshot first with `node scripts/inspect-backroadbum-business.mjs --check`, record product identity and an operation guard before each submission, and read back after uncertainty rather than replaying a mutation. Keep the SKU, Regular/Sale prices, manual availability, gallery and authored content unchanged unless they are separately intended edits. Do not change a word merely to manufacture a content draft, write `offSale`/`pageClaim` directly, reuse a paid-order capability as an admin grant, or call a shopper endpoint to force publication.

## What the rehearsal established
Two original shopper contexts (1440/390) used native Add for two Field Books. Their exact saved intent and quantity survived archive and restored-draft unavailability; after the native publication workaround, the same intent recovered the product name and $24 subtotal. No shopper write request, checkout capability, payment, order or label was created. Checkout admission was closed in the copy, with outbound network unavailable.

All 196 copied plugin rows were compared using `(plugin_id, collection, id)`: 194 unchanged, only the product and publication-pointer rows changed. All 9 Orders (including an existing paid order containing the Field Book), 13 carts, 123 guest capabilities and 13 payment associations were unchanged. Authored CMS content was byte-identical; only the Field Book's native publication metadata changed. The six scoped **live** business/CMS hashes also remain identical. Staff sign-in before cloning necessarily created its own live session and retained the passkey counter; that is not a merchant catalog write or an off-host-auth recovery test.

The disposable service bound copied data over the live data path in its own mount namespace, made the remaining filesystem read-only and hid private configuration/controller state. Browser traffic to the public-looking origin was unconditionally proxied to loopback **inside a different network namespace**; it had no live fallback. Four public images are redacted interaction states, not main/head repair proof: `/pr-proof/product-restore/`. Temporary staff/shopper state, database, uploads and service are removed after retaining aggregate evidence. Historical one-off drivers require the private marker/namespace and completed guard; never run them against the public store or recreate a completed operation.

Reconsider the workaround when the owning issue has a maintainer decision and the applicable implementation is independently qualified. A source merge alone is not a changed signed installation.
