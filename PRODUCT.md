## Product Purpose
A working independent outdoor demo brand and a real merchant proving ground for DinkusKit. Success is an understandable browse → cart → Stripe test payment → receipt → sandbox fulfillment journey and useful framework fixes grounded in that journey.

## Capabilities and Constraints
All purchases are demonstrations: no real charges or physical shipments. Commerce owns price and availability; EmDash owns editorial content. Stripe must fail closed outside test mode. Admin must require strong authentication. Inventory is off until needed. Current installation and checkout status must be reported honestly rather than inferred from a catalog render.

## Product Principles
- Make the next shopping action clear, including a findable route back to the latest browser-bound checkout.
- Let shoppers search gear, collections and reading from one labeled form. Search destinations use published EmDash content and Commerce product visibility, never indexed prices as shopping authority; unavailable search is not a zero-result claim.
- Variant catalog cards say Check options, not the default member's stock state: a sold-out M does not mean L is sold out. This is navigation guidance, never reconstructed family inventory; simple-product cards keep their authoritative stock labels.
- Sized products keep distinct Commerce member identities in the cart; changing a size resets the earlier saved-addition feedback, and sold-out choices stay visible but cannot be added. The live tee has M/L/XL; its original M identity and historical orders remain intact. Selected-offer metadata never borrows a sibling/default SKU. Template PR214 is dogfooded in the theme: older signed payloads retain only page facts without an Offer; simple-product metadata stays intact. Commerce PR260 proposes the missing public member SKU and passes paired source proof, but no new signed plugin is deployed. Restore selected-member Offers only from that authoritative public fact, never labels or CMS content.
- Show authoritative commerce facts, including Orders-assigned receipt numbers, never a second price list or a locally invented order label. The signed Commerce public projection supplies the current customerPays price; do not reconstruct Regular/Sale comparisons from CMS or old cart data.
- After a product is saved to the cart, offer a keyboard-reachable View cart link beside the confirmation. Invalid quantities and failed saves must not show that saved-cart invitation; do not move focus or navigate automatically.
- Keep product inspection connected to onward browsing: use published EmDash collection membership for related gear, not invented bundles or purchase-history claims. Exclude the current item and duplicates; Commerce still owns every displayed product fact.
- Keep test instructions findable and separate from editorial storytelling. Shipping and Returns keep their EmDash-authored policy text, with direct links to the test guide and browser-bound last checkout; do not make shoppers hunt for a receipt in the footer.
- Give field notes a complete reading path: practical EmDash-authored advice, another note and an unobtrusive way back to the gear. Never imply the fictional merchandise has been field-tested.
- Let shoppers reopen a cart item's product page to inspect its details without searching the shop again. Use only Commerce's current page link matched to a published EmDash entry; missing destinations remain plain text. A sized line leads to its parent page with its exact current Commerce member selected, without changing saved identities or quantities. Reload and copied selection URLs retain that option; a stale, foreign or duplicate item request asks for a fresh choice instead of silently substituting a sibling. Canonical product URLs stay query-free.
- When Commerce cannot name a cart item, use neutral numbered labels instead of a catalog UUID: Item 1 while details are unknown, Unavailable item 1 after a missing/unlisted response. These are positions, not product names or new identities; keep IDs and quantities unchanged, and recover real names only from Commerce. Unpublishing and republishing the same native product must preserve its saved cart intent.
- Keep cart edits honest: an invalid quantity is an unsaved draft, not permission to purchase the previous quantity. Explain the correction beside its field, suppress stale totals and block checkout until every draft is corrected or removed.
- Let shoppers print or save the authorized paid receipt without creating another checkout or exporting its browser capability. A saved copy is a static record, not online recovery or a live fulfillment view. Verify existing-receipt printing independently of new-checkout admission, against the signed Commerce projection; absent legacy fields stay absent. Native and hosted label reprints must reuse the existing label rather than buy another one.
- Treat failure and recovery as part of the product. Do not collect sign-in emails when delivery is unavailable; offer the browser-bound receipt with its same-browser/latest-checkout limits. Mail admission and new-checkout admission are separate, and neither invents a shopper session.
- Keep operational evidence public-safe and truthful. Activity reads only allowlisted metadata from retained session records and controller state; distinguish observed prompt-to-final windows from controller outcomes, cached token processing from unique text, and API-equivalent estimates from actual billed costs. Missing accounting stays unavailable; transcripts and credentials never become dashboard data.
